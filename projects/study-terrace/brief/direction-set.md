# Direction set — Back bedroom to home office
Project: `projects/study-terrace` · 6 directions · gate: `direction-set` (awaiting approval)

## 0. The room, restated as constraints on the set

| Fact | What it forbids | What it invites |
|---|---|---|
| 3.4 across × 3.8 along, 12.4 m², h 2.65 | Nothing that eats floor. No island-plus-perimeter-joinery scheme. | Perimeter thickness; one central object. |
| Desk 1.6 × 0.9 = 1.44 m², 12% of the floor, and it stays | Any direction whose headline move needs the desk gone or shrunk. | The desk as the room's one piece of furniture, given a proper station. |
| Chimney breast 1400 wide, 350 proud, alcoves 1000 × **350** each | Wardrobes, desks or seating in the alcoves — 350 mm is too shallow. | 350 mm is exactly book and box-file depth (box file = 320). The alcoves are shelf-shaped and nothing else. |
| Window on w8 only; sill 820; radiator 1000 wide directly under it, fixed | Backing the desk onto the window. Boxing the radiator in. | The window wall is the one wall that cannot be storage. Make it something else or leave it. |
| Backdrop rule (P2): the wall the sitter's **back** faces is what the camera sees | The window can never be behind the sitter. | Only w1, w2 or the far wall can be the backdrop. Each direction picks one and designs it. |
| Later let | Structural or heritage loss. | Fitted joinery and new sockets are assets, not damage — "reversible" here means non-destructive, not demountable. |

Door swing occupies the quadrant x 0.75–1.53, y 0–0.78. Every plan below is checked clear of it.

## 2. The seven axes, direction by direction

### D1 — Make Good · tier A · conservation-first, culturally native
- **Plan / zoning** — Desk against the entrance wall in the party-wall corner (x 1.7–3.3, y 0.15–1.05), sitter at y ≈ 1.4 facing the desk, back to the room. Camera looks the full length of the room at the reopened hearth 2.4 m away. Reading chair off the left alcove. The centre of the room stays empty, as it was when it was a bedroom.
- **Ceiling** — Lath and plaster retained, hairline crack stitched and made good, flat distemper. Original pendant kept, plain paper shade. Picture rail and frieze intact.
- **Floor** — Boards lifted only where they squeak, gaps filled, sanded and hard-waxed to natural pine. One large antique wool rug.
- **Seating** — Client's task chair re-upholstered in wool + a free-standing low Victorian reading chair at the hearth. Nothing built.
- **Storage** — Two secondhand oak bookcases, one per alcove, ~250 deep so they sit inside the 350 alcove line. A small chest beside the desk. All of it moves out in an afternoon.
- **Lighting architecture** — No new circuits. Pendant plus plug-in: desk lamp, and a floor lamp positioned as the call key light. New sockets fed in painted skirting-level trunking.
- **Programme** — Unchanged: a study that is also the quiet reading room of the house.
- **Cost of restraint** — 350 mm of alcove and £0 of joinery means the 6 m of books arrive as furniture, and furniture in a 12.4 m² room is bulk.

### D2 — Two Alcoves, One Hearth · tier B · native to the house
- **Plan / zoning** — Desk centred on the breast (x 0.9–2.5, y 1.65–2.55), pulled 900 mm off the far wall so the sitter sits **in front of** the joinery with their back to it. 900 mm clear each side, 1.65 m of forecourt between the desk and the door. Window falls on the sitter's left at 90°.
- **Ceiling** — Ceiling and the frieze above the picture rail carried in one deep tone down to the rail; joinery stops at rail height so the room keeps a continuous top edge. Pendant swapped for a flush period fitting, since the desk is now underneath it.
- **Floor** — Boards stained near-black and matt-lacquered; no rug. The joinery is the event.
- **Seating** — Task chair, plus a second chair opposite across the desk. It is a *partners'* desk; this is the only direction that uses it as one.
- **Storage** — Full-height fitted joinery both alcoves: cupboards to 750 (box files at 320 fit the 350 depth exactly), open shelves above. 4 shelves × 0.95 × 2 = **7.6 lin m**, against 6 required.
- **Lighting architecture** — Joinery-integrated LED under each shelf head, two wall lights at picture-rail height, one fixed key light on the window wall aimed at the sitter. Fused spur feeding sockets in the joinery plinth.
- **Programme** — Library-study for one, with a facing seat for a second.

### D3 — The Warm Lining · tier C · the envelope answer
- **Plan / zoning** — The external wall becomes an inhabited thickness ~400 deep: bench at y 0.2–1.4, radiator left fully open at y 1.4–2.4, second bench at y 2.4–3.4. Desk floats as an island parallel to the window (x 1.2–2.1, y 1.3–2.9); sitter on the party-wall side facing the daylight across 1.2 m. Alcoves left open and empty.
- **Ceiling** — Swept clear. Pendant removed and blanked, ceiling repaired and finished in a warm pale distemper. The only ceiling-line event is a shallow curtain box at the window.
- **Floor** — Boards lifted, joists insulated, boards relaid and draught-sealed, then wall-to-wall wool cord carpet on felt. The largest single heat-loss fix available in the room.
- **Seating** — Two built-in window benches flanking the radiator + task chair. The room gains two places to sit that are not the desk.
- **Storage** — A continuous shelf at picture-rail height running three walls (≈ 8 lin m, straight through the alcoves) plus lidded boxes inside the benches. Nothing at eye level; the room stays visually empty.
- **Lighting architecture** — All light from the walls. Swing-arm brass sconces at each bench and over the desk, no ceiling fitting at all. Sockets in the bench plinths.
- **Programme** — Office plus winter reading room; this becomes the warm room of the house.
- **Flag** — Slim secondary glazing to the sash (lift-out, no alteration to the original). The insulated lining must be a vapour-open build-up (battens + wood fibre + lime), and it takes ~75 mm off the 3.4 m width — absorbed in the bench depth. It is the one direction that a landlord should be told about explicitly.

### D4 — The Call Room · tier C · programme change
- **Plan / zoning** — Split front-of-camera / back-of-house. Camera axis runs **across** the room: sitter's back to the acoustically lined party wall, desk between sitter and window (x 1.4–2.3, y 1.3–2.9), 1.1 m of chair zone behind. Daylight arrives frontally from 2.4 m, diffused by a blind. The whole far wall is service.
- **Ceiling** — A suspended fabric-wrapped acoustic raft, 1.6 × 1.2, soffit at 2.40 over the desk, carrying the lighting. Remainder of the ceiling flat and absorbent.
- **Floor** — Acoustic underlay and sheet linoleum on a ply raft over the boards. Seamless, quiet, one colour, entirely liftable.
- **Seating** — One properly specified task chair and one folding chair. No soft seating anywhere — this is a working booth and admits it.
- **Storage** — Closed low cabinet run across the entire far wall, 400 deep × 700 high, passing straight over the breast and flattening it; two shallow book shelves above = **6.8 lin m**. Alcoves become cable and service voids behind the run.
- **Lighting architecture** — A rig. Bi-colour key panel on the raft, fill from the far side, a wash on the backdrop wall, one scene control. Floor box plus dado trunking on a dedicated circuit.
- **Programme** — Broadcast/call studio that is also an office. Secondary glazing here is bought for acoustics and gets the thermal win for free.

### D5 — The Paper Room · tier B · programme change
- **Plan / zoning** — Two working heights. Seated station at the entrance end (desk x 1.4–3.0, y 1.3–2.2), sitter at y ≈ 0.9 with their back to the entrance wall, facing the chimney; standing lay-out ledge 400 deep × 1050 high on the party wall (x 3.0–3.4, y 2.4–3.6) butting the desk end as an L; A1 plan chest in front of the left alcove (x 0.2–1.3, y 2.9–3.7), its top a landing surface. Clear of the door swing by 520 mm.
- **Ceiling** — Flat white, crack repaired, picture rail kept as a hanging rail; surface conduit expressed rather than hidden.
- **Floor** — Boards painted in a pale grey deck paint to bounce light off the single window. Hard chair mat, no soft floor.
- **Seating** — Task chair at the desk, high stool at the ledge. Two postures, no lounging.
- **Storage** — Flat-paper typology: plan chest for drawings, adjustable steel shelving screwed into both alcoves for box files and books (~6.5 lin m), full cork/hessian pinboard across the entrance wall. Unscrews and leaves fixings only.
- **Lighting architecture** — Indirect. Two surface-mounted linear luminaires bounced off the white ceiling, an anglepoise on the desk, and a wall-mounted uplight doing the call key. Sockets at the ledge and desk.
- **Programme** — Drawing office / archive. The room's job becomes making and keeping paper; the calls happen against a wall of live work.

### D6 — The Guest Study · tier A/B · programme change, letting-aware
- **Plan / zoning** — Desk offset to the window side of the breast (x 0.5–2.1, y 2.55–3.45), sitter at y ≈ 2.2 with their back to the plain entrance wall. Daybed 1900 × 900 along the party wall (x 2.5–3.4, y 0.9–2.8), which is **behind the camera** — the bed is present in the room and never in a call. Ceiling curtain track on the line x = 2.4 draws it out of the study entirely.
- **Ceiling** — Repaired and pale, carrying a slim ceiling curtain track and a second pendant over the bed zone. The ceiling is what makes the room two rooms.
- **Floor** — Bound-edge seagrass matting wall to wall, sheepskin at the bed.
- **Seating** — The daybed with bolsters doubles as the room's sofa; task chair at the desk. The only direction with a proper place to sit back.
- **Storage** — Free-standing painted armoire in the right alcove holding guest bedding and files, wall-hung shelves on the breast face and in the left alcove (1.4 × 3 + 1.0 × 3 = **7.2 lin m**). Nothing built in.
- **Lighting architecture** — Two scenes. Desk task light and work pendant; bedside swing-arm and soft pendant for guest use. Clip-on key light that stows.
- **Programme** — Office by day, guest bedroom by night — and the room stays countable as a bedroom when the property is let, which is worth real money.

**Honest notes on the ~ marks and the near misses**

- *D1–D6 lighting* — both are plug-in-led with no rewired architecture. D6 adds a second pendant, a ceiling track and two-scene switching, but they are cousins. They separate on the other six axes.
- *D2–D5 floor* — dark-stained boards vs pale-painted boards is the same technique in two colours. Marked ~ deliberately rather than claimed as a difference.
- *D3–D6 floor* — wool cord carpet vs seagrass matting: both continuous soft floors. Marked ~.
- *D3 and D4 share the party wall as backdrop*, and *D5 and D6 share the entrance wall*. This is forced: with the window on w8 there are only three legal backdrop walls for six directions. In each case the plan logic is different enough to score ✔ on **P** — D3 is a perimeter-thickness plan with a floating island, D4 is a zoned front-of-camera/back-of-house plan with the far wall given over to service; D5 is a two-height drawing office, D6 is a curtained two-programme room. If the client disagrees, the pairs still clear the threshold at 6 and 6 without **P**.

## 5. Spread check

- **Budget** — A: D1, D6. B: D2, D5. C: D3, D4. Two directions are buildable on a small budget without apology, and D1 is genuinely cheap.
- **Programme** — four of six change what the room is FOR: D3 (winter room), D4 (studio), D5 (drawing office), D6 (guest room). D1 and D2 keep the programme and change the quality.
- **Register** — D2 is culturally native: alcove joinery either side of a chimney breast is what this house would have done to itself. D1 is the conservation-first option that repairs rather than redesigns.
- **Buildability** — every headline move checked against the plan. Nothing proposed is wider than the room; the desk is placed and clearance-checked in all six; the alcoves are never asked to be deeper than 350; the radiator is left uncovered in all six; the sash is repaired or secondary-glazed, never replaced.

## 6. What the room probably wants — **D2, Two Alcoves, One Hearth**

Not because it is the prettiest, but because it is the only direction where the room's awkwardness and the client's needs are the same problem. The alcoves are 350 mm deep: too shallow for anything but books and box files, and books and box files are exactly what 6 linear metres of brief asks for. Filling them costs no floor area in a room that has 12.4 m² and must give 1.44 of it to a desk that cannot leave. Pulling the desk 900 off the joinery solves P2 with a piece of cabinetwork instead of a lighting rig — the sitter's back is a wall of books, which needs no dimming and looks the same in February as in June. It leaves 1.65 m of clear floor between the desk and the door, which is the difference between a small room and a cramped one. And it spends the budget on something screwed to the building: when the flat is let, fitted alcove joinery in a Victorian back bedroom reads as original, and adds value rather than needing explanation.

**Argue against it, honestly:**
- If the client really is on camera most of the working day, **D4** is the answer to the job they actually do, and D2 only half-solves the sound.
- If the room is unusable from November to March — solid brick, single glazing, one radiator — then **D3** fixes the thing that is actually wrong, and every other direction is decoration on a cold room.
- If the budget is tight, **D1** is not a consolation prize. A repaired sash, a waxed floor and a working hearth in a 12.4 m² room is a better room than most of what money buys, and it is the option clients are almost never offered.

D2 is the recommendation. The decision is the client's.
